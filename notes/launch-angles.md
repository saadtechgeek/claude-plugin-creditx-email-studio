# Launch angles: CreditX Advisor waitlist

Each angle is one idea, so each email can be read in under a minute. All of them follow `brand/voice.md`: no promised approval, no quoted numbers as certain, no score promises, and every email says "CreditX Advisor suggests cards. The card issuer makes the approval decision."

## Angles for everyone

1. **Skip the comparing.** Choosing a card means sorting through dozens of options. CreditX Advisor narrows them down to a few that fit you.
2. **A friend who knows cards.** A calm, honest second opinion. No hype and no pressure.
3. **Fees stated up front.** We say plainly whether a card has an annual fee, so there are no surprises.

## Building credit

1. **Start from where you are.** Cards made for building credit exist, and we suggest ones that fit your situation. Talk about the group's situation, never a number.
2. **No shame, just options.** Plain, kind words. Never "bad credit" or "poor credit".
3. **What to look for in a first card.** Is there a deposit? Is there an annual fee? Does it report your payments? A simple checklist, with no promises about results.

Cards we can mention: Fresh Start Secured, Second Chance Builder, Stepping Stone Starter, Student Builder (check the annual fee each time).

## Seeking rewards

1. **Match the card to your spending.** Groceries, fuel, bills or streaming each have a card that fits. Cash back typically depends on where you spend.
2. **Keep it simple.** One flat cash-back card suits people who don't want to think about categories. FlatCash Everyday is the example.
3. **Carrying a balance?** A balance-transfer card may help, but there's a transfer fee and an interest rate (APR) afterwards, and we say so. Cash Cushion is the example.

## Premium spenders

1. **One card or a few.** Do you want a single top card, or separate cards for travel, dining and shopping? Explain how to decide.
2. **Is the annual fee worth it?** An honest look at when a higher annual fee fits and when it doesn't. We never say a card is worth it for everyone.
3. **Travel or dining first.** Pick the card that matches your biggest spend. Examples are Voyager Elite, Globetrotter Plus, DineDelight Signature and Summit Reserve.

## Suggested sequence (for the planning step)

1. Welcome and what CreditX does (everyone).
2. The group-specific angle (one per group).
3. A fees-and-fit email (everyone, includes the "who decides" line).

## Things to avoid

- Reading anyone's score back to them, or hinting we looked it up.
- Words like guaranteed, pre-approved, act now, fix, repair or erase.
- Naming real banks or card brands.
- Stating a rate, limit or fee as what the reader will get. Use "from" or "rates vary".
