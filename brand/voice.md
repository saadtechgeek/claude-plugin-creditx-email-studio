# CreditX Advisor: brand voice

CreditX Advisor is a made-up AI advisor that suggests credit cards to fit a person's credit score and spending. We write to busy working adults, aged 28 to 52, who want to pick the right card without comparing dozens. They sit at three credit stages: building credit (under 670), seeking rewards (670-769) and premium spenders (770 and up).

## 1. Our voice in three words

**Clear, calm, fair.** We sound like a friend who knows cards. We never talk down to anyone, never hype, and never use fear or urgency to push a reader. Credit can feel stressful, so we write for a worried reader.

How that shows up:
- One idea per email, so a busy reader gets it in under a minute.
- Be honest about fees: if we mention a card with an annual fee, we say so in the same sentence or the next.
- We never name or criticize real banks or card brands. CreditX is a made-up advisor, and we stay on our own products.

## 2. Words we use

- suggest, compare, fit, match, options, may, typically, depends
- Plain terms: "annual fee", "interest rate", "cash back", "credit score"
- "building credit" for people whose scores are low
- Contractions like "you're" and "it's" are fine. They sound natural, and the required footer already uses one.

## 3. Words we avoid

- guaranteed, pre-approved, instant approval, free money, no-risk, fix your credit, erase, repair, best card ever, act now, last chance
- Jargon without a reason: write "interest rate (APR)" instead of "APR" alone, and don't use "utilization" or "tradeline"
- Shaming words about people's credit: bad credit, poor credit, subprime

## 4. Claims we must never make

1. **No promised approval.** Never say "you'll be approved", "guaranteed" or "pre-approved". CreditX suggests cards, and the issuer decides.
2. **No quoted numbers as certain.** Never state a rate, limit or fee as what the reader will get. Say "rates vary" or "from", and point to the issuer's terms.
3. **No score promises.** Never say a score will rise by a set amount or in a set time.
4. **No credit repair claims.** Never say a card fixes, repairs or erases bad credit.
5. **No reading someone's score back to them.** Emails never state a person's score or imply we looked it up. They speak to the group's situation, not to a number.
6. **Always say who decides.** Include somewhere in each email: "CreditX Advisor suggests cards. The card issuer makes the approval decision."
